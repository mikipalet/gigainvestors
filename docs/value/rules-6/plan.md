## Accounting and edge decisions recorded BEFORE evaluation

1. NI includes SBC; retain that expense without another deduction. OCF adds it back; subtract SBC on the OCF path. Parent NI already belongs to common owners; consolidated adjustments retain parent allocation.
2. All operating firms use the median of the last five annual owner-earnings/revenue margins multiplied by latest annual revenue or complete newer TTM revenue. No quality eligibility filter and no minimum of dollar observations. Five distinct consecutive annual observations, finite earnings and positive finite denominators are required. Missing data is not zero or permission to select older profitable years.
3. Negative/zero median margin gives no positive going-concern earnings valuation. A small positive margin is used as-is (no arbitrary fitted floor); all arithmetic must be finite. Individual loss years remain in the median. Zero/nonpositive current revenue cannot support this model. Existing unavailable-valuation continuity remains explicit, never a claim of a new computed value.
4. Revenue collapse immediately reduces scale. The existing three-year revenue-decline zero-growth override and cyclical safety discount remain. Cyclicals use the same through-cycle five-year margin including losses. Five years may not cover an entire economic cycle; disclose that limitation rather than fit a company exception.
5. Financial tangible-book/ROE and holding-company NAV methods remain separate and unchanged.
6. Annual and TTM owner-earnings bridges use the same maintenance estimator: capex less estimated growth investment, floored at min(capex, D&A), or an evidenced same-period maintenance judgement. A trailing observation uses annual PPE/revenue context and the increase from latest annual revenue to trailing revenue; these overlapping twelve-month windows are disclosed. Never inherit an annual maintenance judgement/lease cash amount as a reported TTM flow. Incomplete TTM revenue falls back to annual scale.
7. Working-capital mismatch: NI bridge omits reinvestment in working capital while OCF includes actual changes. A balance-sheet delta is not a reliable cash-flow adjustment (acquisitions, FX, noncash changes), and required maintenance working capital is not identical to total growth investment. Investigate available explicit cash-flow tags; do not invent a corpus-wide adjustment from ambiguous vendor balances.
8. Lease double counting: reported financing principal is an additional owner cash outflow; operating lease payments already in OCF must not be deducted twice. Existing leaseCash is documented as capitalized repayments, with explicit financing tags in issuer parsers. Country-based ROU/D&A and the 20%-liability proxy lack issuer-wide separation of ROU depreciation from owned-asset maintenance. Do not guess a new universal percentage; record unresolved scope and fix any proven classification error.
9. Excess-cash interest overlap: crediting cash while capitalizing its income can double count. Cash-specific after-tax income is necessary; generic interest income can belong to operating lending, different balances or taxable jurisdictions. Do not infer a risk-free yield or treat missing interest as known zero. Document absent attribution; fix only cases with explicit compatible source inputs.

Sources: Berkshire 1986 owner-earnings discussion (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1986.html); IAS 7 cash-flow classifications and noncash adjustments (https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/). These support accounting distinctions, not a newly fitted investment rule.
